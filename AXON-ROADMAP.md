## THE WIN CONDITION (what "god-tier" actually means — measurable, not a feeling)

You don't win by *feeling* relentless. You win by making four things true, with dates and receipts:

1. **Real product** — Redoubt Sideline is deployed, not a demo. Someone who isn't you can open it and run a real workflow.
2. **Real users** — at least one youth/HS football program uses it for a real season. Not "interested." *Using.*
3. **Real data / validation** — head-impact and protocol-adherence data from actual games. Numbers you collected, not slides you made.
4. **External gatekeeper** — an adult institution that isn't your mom vouches for it: a competition placement, a grant, an accelerator, a pilot MOU, or press.

Hit 3 of 4 and no admissions officer on earth files you under "another kid with good grades." Hit 4 and you're a recruited *builder*, not an applicant.

## THE TRAP THAT KILLS IT (read twice)

**Do not build a concussion _diagnostic_. Build a head-impact _monitoring + protocol_ system.**

- You cannot validate "this athlete is concussed" without clinical data and IRB access you will not get as a solo teenager. Claiming it is false, and a false negative on a real field endangers a real kid.
- Your repo already made the right call: *"documentation log, not medical device."* That maturity is itself a signal to serious people. Do not undo it to sound cooler.
- Correct, defensible, still-impressive framing: **"A sideline tool that logs head-impact events and drives return-to-play protocol adherence, so trainers and coaches follow the steps that already exist."** That is a real unmet need, it is legal, it is safe, and it is buildable by you.
- Every time you're tempted to say "detects concussions," say **"monitors impacts and enforces protocol"** instead. It's more true and it makes you more credible, not less.

## THE LADDER (climb top-down; don't skip rungs)

**RUNG 1 — Make it real (deployable, honest, usable)**
- Redoubt Sideline works end-to-end on a phone on an actual sideline. Offline-tolerant. No jargon a volunteer coach can't follow.
- Data export/import is trustworthy (you already hardened `importData` — keep that rigor).
- Scope the claim on the product itself: monitoring + protocol, not diagnosis.

**RUNG 2 — Get one real user (the hardest rung — most founders die here)**
- Land ONE youth or HS football program for one season. A local team, your future position coach's network, a trainer you interview.
- One real team beats ten demos and a hundred "that's cool" texts. This is the rung that separates founders from hobbyists.

**RUNG 3 — Collect real data + show it works**
- A season of logged impact events + protocol steps followed. Even n=1 team is a story if the data is real.
- Write it up honestly: what happened, what the tool caught, what it missed. Founders who report limitations read as *more* credible.

**RUNG 4 — Get an external gatekeeper to vouch**
Pick 1–2, don't spray:
- **Competitions:** Regeneron STS / ISEF (if timing works senior year), Conrad Challenge, Diamond Challenge, local pitch comps.
- **Grants / programs:** youth-founder grants, Neo, small pre-seed for student founders.
- **Institutional pilot:** a signed MOU or written endorsement from the team/league using it.
- **Press:** local paper → sports-safety outlets. Real deployment is what earns coverage.
- **IP:** a **provisional patent** is cheap (~$65–150 filing) and gives you "patent-pending" honestly, if there's a genuine novel mechanism. Don't file on nothing.
